abstract: This article introduces a novel framework that effectively resolves multipath
  interference (MPI) in time-of-flight (ToF) depth measurements by utilizing a single-shot
  time-compressive complementary metal–oxide– semiconductor (CMOS) image sensor, a
  U-Net-based neural network, and joint end-to-end optimization. Conventional indirect
  ToF (iToF) methods inevitably suffer from MPI artifacts because each detection tap
  integrates incoming photons over a wide exposure window, making it difficult to
  isolate the direct reflection component from multiple reflections or scattering.
  Our approach, termed coded ToF (cToF), builds upon the iToF principle but employs
  coded exposure patterns to capture the scene response in a single shot. This time-compressive
  acquisition allows for the effective separation of MPI components, achieving direct
  ToF (dToF)- like high accuracy using a low-cost iToF-based sensor. Furthermore,
  the reconstruction network and the coded shutter are jointly optimized in an end-to-end
  framework to maximize depth estimation accuracy while explicitly considering shutter
  control constraints.
